# 🏴‍☠️ Pirates of Tokyo Bay: New Member Orientation Manual

Welcome aboard! This manual exists to answer 90% of the questions new members ask, before you have to ask them. Read this manual first, then ask your mentor, then ask in Slack.

## Mission, Vision, and Values

- Mission: To get everyone, regardless of their native language, to laugh!
- Vision: To be seen as the global representative of quality Japan-based improv with the support of a multicultural fanbase, and as a group that enriches its members' lives through personal and professional growth on and off stage.

### Turning Values into Actions

**Got Your Back (ついてるから大丈夫)**

- On stage and off, we support our members: step in when a scene is struggling, and guide new members so they can do their best.
- Every member has different strengths, and combining them is what being a Pirate means!

**Family (家族)**

- Many members live far from their own families, so we support each other and aim to be people each other can trust, like family.
- And like any healthy family, respect each person's boundaries and comfort levels in personal engagement (sharing personal information, physical contact, activities). When in doubt, ask first.

### As a Member

1. Courtesy matters, even among close friends.
2. Know when to play and when to focus.
3. Stay aware that people are watching: you represent the Pirates.

### The Bus Test

One reason you are here is the Bus Test: "would we enjoy sitting next to you on a long bus ride?" Every Pirate passed the audition by unanimous vote, and everyone passed this test. Keep passing it after you join: be someone your fellow Pirates want to be around.

### Stage Time vs Show Quality

- We do our best to get every member on stage.
- But stage time is not fixed. It fluctuates every month based on practice attendance, language, and skill needs.
- The core of every show is its overall quality. Do not count your minutes; make the show great. A great show is a win for every member on the roster.

### How We Talk About Ourselves

- We are a Japan-based troupe in Tokyo (Kanto) performing English and Japanese improv comedy. We are not an overseas group, not an American group, and we do not perform "American-style" comedy.
- We are neither a foreigners' group nor a Japanese group. We promote what we do, not who we are. We are the only troupe that switches between Japanese and English in a single show, all unscripted.
- What we teach is improv: the skill (training) and the art (shows). We are not an English or Japanese language school. Language learners are welcome, as audience members enjoying the show.
- For the exact wording → use the approved strings in the Approved Language section below.

# 🎭 Multilingual Performance Framework

## Know the Room

Every audience mixes people who speak only Japanese, only English, both, or a little of each: international couples, friend groups, tourists, and language learners. Guests never need both languages. Our mission is to get everyone laughing, so nobody gets left behind.

## Language Balance

- Aim for roughly 50% Japanese and 50% English across the show.
- Make each scene's premise clear to both language audiences during the setup.

## On-Stage Techniques

- Pantomime and physical comedy: keep movement in the scene so the comedy works without words.
- Gibberish (デタラメ語): turn the language barrier itself into the game.
- Simple language: short sentences, everyday words, clear delivery. Use localized English and Japanese instead of building jokes on complex vocabulary.

## Escaping the Talking-Head Trap

Two people standing and talking in one language leaves half the room behind. When you feel the trap:

1. Add movement → show the situation physically
2. Switch languages, or reflect the key point back in the other language
3. If it is still heavy, edit: cut the scene or step in

Got Your Back applies to language too. If a fellow Pirate is struggling in their second language, step in and help.

# 📢 Marketing, Social Media & Styling Protocols

## Approved Language

- Approved strings: "English and Japanese improv comedy" in English, and 「日本語と英語で楽しめる即興コメディ」 in Japanese (short Japanese label: 「日本語＆英語」).
- Banned: never describe the group with the word "Bilingual" or 「バイリンガル」 in any post, flyer, or conversation. Always use the approved strings above.
- Why: we used to use it, but it made guests think they need both languages to enjoy the show. "Japanese & English" tells people that either language is enough.
- Proof points you may cite: Winner of the Peatix Community Award 2026 (Creative Arts Community, selected from over 250 communities), TripAdvisor #4 Theater & Concerts in Shibuya, performing since 2010.

## Posting Rules

- Share show and group info any way you like. As long as you follow these rules, promotion is always welcome.
- When posting in both languages, Japanese always comes first, English second.
- Link to piratesoftokyobay.com/shows. Never paste /tickets or Fienta URLs.
- Show paths and choices with text arrows: e.g. details → /shows
- Do not use long dash characters in copy. Use colons, commas, or parentheses.
- Official accounts: @piratestokyo on Instagram, X, and TikTok.

# ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (Internal Ops)

**1. Where does the group keep everything?**

Just 3 tools. 1) Google Sheets: attendance (past and future), show history, finances, goals, the game list, merchandise tracking, and contacts. 2) Google Docs: practice notes, mission and vision, and other working documents. 3) Slack: all communication (group, private, silly, and translation requests too). Links to the Sheets and Docs are pinned in Slack, and your mentor sends your Slack invite.

**2. What is the engagement score?**

Points earned from attendance at every group-wide event and activity: Yes = +1, No = -1, Maybe = 0, Helping at a show = +2. Scores feed every decision: who performs (and how much), who travels for overseas shows or corporate workshops, and who becomes an officer (leader). Visible data keeps decisions transparent and builds trust. Separately, operational roles (SEO, social media, photos, design, accounting) are tasks you can take on that match your day job or hobby, so you build skills the group needs, and the skills you build here work in real life too (they can go on your resume or profile).

**3. How do I order my Pirates shirt?**

Shirts are handled at piratesoftokyobay.com/membershirt. Pick from the current stock list first; if no size or color fits, order one from the same page. One shirt per Pirate, for now.

**4. What if I have to miss a practice?**

Update the attendance sheet yourself; that is the official notice. For last-minute changes, also give the group a heads-up in Slack. Then catch up on what you missed in the practice notes on Google Docs (the Living Practice Note, our shared agenda and record of every practice).

**5. How is show casting decided?**

Objectively and fairly, based on data. The inputs are the attendance record and engagement score, language balance, skill needs, and what the show needs to be great. The record decides, not guesses or favorites. Casting is announced in Slack; reply right away if you cannot perform.

**6. Who do I ask when I have a question?**

In this order: this manual → your mentor → Slack. Every new member gets a mentor, a current Pirate with a high engagement score who volunteered for you. This manual and the group's templates are your version of "just google it."
